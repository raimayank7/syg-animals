Lion

Panthera leo · Mammal · Carnivore

Lions are the only cats that live in family groups. A pride is made up of related females, their cubs and a few males. Adult males have a mane. The females do most of the hunting, working together. The only wild lions outside Africa live in and around Gir National Park in Gujarat.

What the lion eats

Lions hunt medium and large animals: zebra, wildebeest and buffalo in Africa, and chital, sambar and nilgai in Gir, where they also take cattle. The lionesses fan out, creep close and rush together. Lions also steal kills from hyenas and leopards whenever they can. At a kill the adult males usually eat first, then the females, and the cubs last.

How it lives

A pride rests for most of the day and becomes active around dusk. The males guard the territory, and their roar carries for several kilometres on a still night. Cubs are born spotted and are hidden for the first weeks. Any lioness in the pride with milk may suckle them. Daughters usually stay in the pride for life, while young males are pushed out and must win a pride of their own.

How to tell it apart

A grown male with a mane cannot be mistaken. Lionesses and young lions are plain tawny with no stripes or rosettes, and both sexes have a dark tuft at the tip of the tail, which no other cat has. Asiatic lions have a fold of skin along the belly, and the males have a thinner mane, so the ears usually show.

The lion and people

The lion appears on the State Emblem of India, taken from the Ashoka pillar at Sarnath. By the early 1900s only a small number were left in Gir. Protection has brought them back, and lions now spread well beyond the park into farmland, where herders and lions live close together.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Carnivora
  5. Family Felidae
  6. Genus Panthera
  7. Species Panthera leo

Other members of the family Felidae on this site: cheetah, domestic cat, jaguar, leopard, snow leopard, tiger.

More distant relatives in the order Carnivora: brown bear, dog, giant panda, polar bear, red fox, red panda, sloth bear, spotted hyena, walrus, wolf.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The lion is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
