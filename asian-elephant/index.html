Asian elephant

Elephas maximus · Mammal · Herbivore

The Asian elephant is smaller than its African cousins, with smaller ears and a rounded back. Only some males grow long tusks. Its trunk is nose, hand and drinking straw in one. India has more wild Asian elephants than any other country.

What the Asian elephant eats

Asian elephants eat grass, bamboo, leaves, bark, roots and fruit, and they are very fond of crops such as sugarcane, paddy and bananas. An adult eats for most of the day and night and drinks a great deal of water. The trunk does the work of a hand, a nose and a hose at once.

How it lives

Herds are groups of related females and calves, led by an old female. Adult males live mostly alone. From time to time a male comes into musth, a state in which he is restless and aggressive. A single calf is born after about 22 months and is looked after by its mother and its 'aunts'. Elephants bathe often and throw dust over their skin to guard it from sun and insects.

How to tell it apart

Smaller ears, a rounded or level back and a forehead with two domes separate it from the African elephant. The trunk ends in a single 'finger'. Only some males carry large tusks. A male without tusks is called a makhna, and females have at most short tushes that barely show.

The Asian elephant and people

The elephant is India's national heritage animal and has a place in festivals, temples and stories everywhere in the country. India started Project Elephant in 1992. The biggest problem today is space. Elephants follow old routes that are now cut by farms, roads and railway lines, and both people and elephants die in the meetings that result.

Is it in danger?

The Asian elephant is listed as Endangered. The species faces a very high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
