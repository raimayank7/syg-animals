Patterns to remember

  • Calf: large plant-eaters and sea mammals, such as cattle, elephants, giraffes, whales and dolphins.
  • Cub: big cats, bears and foxes.
  • Pup: dogs, wolves, seals, bats, mice and sharks.
  • Kit: small furry animals such as rabbits, foxes and beavers.
  • Joey: all marsupials, including kangaroos and koalas.
  • Chick: most birds. A few have their own word, such as cygnet for a swan, duckling for a duck, owlet for an owl and squab for a pigeon.
  • Hatchling: reptiles that come out of eggs, such as turtles, snakes and crocodiles.
  • Tadpole: frogs and toads, while they still live in water.

Names that surprise people

  • A baby platypus is often called a puggle, a word first used for the young of its relative, the echidna.
  • A young pigeon is a squab, and a young swan is a cygnet, from the old French word for swan.
  • A young salmon changes its name as it grows: fry, then parr in the river, then smolt when it heads for the sea.
  • Insects have names for each stage: a butterfly starts as a caterpillar, and a bee or ant as a larva.
  • A baby kangaroo and a baby koala are both joeys, because both grow up in a pouch.

How to use this list

The table gives the word most often used in English for the young of each animal. Tap an animal to read about how its young are born and raised: some can run within the hour, and others are helpless for months. Where two words are in use, both are shown. Teachers can read out the animal and ask the class for the baby name, or turn it round and ask which animals have a "calf" or a "pup". Words for male and female animals, such as bull and cow or cock and hen, are a different set and are not listed here.
