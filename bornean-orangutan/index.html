Bornean orangutan

Pongo pygmaeus · Mammal · Herbivore

Orangutans are large red-haired apes that live in trees. The name means "person of the forest" in Malay. They are mostly solitary, and a mother looks after each young for many years, longer than almost any other animal.

What the Bornean orangutan eats

Orangutans live mainly on fruit, and have a special liking for figs and the strong-smelling durian. When fruit is scarce they fall back on leaves, bark, flowers and insects. They carry a map of the forest in their heads and remember which trees fruit at which time.

How it lives

The orangutan is the largest animal that lives in trees, and it rarely comes to the ground. Adults live mostly alone. A young orangutan stays with its mother for many years, longer than almost any other animal, learning what to eat and how to build a nest. Mothers have a baby only once in several years.

How to tell it apart

Long reddish-orange hair and very long arms make an orangutan easy to know. Grown males develop wide cheek pads and a throat pouch, and their booming 'long call' carries far through the forest. The Sumatran orangutan is paler, with a longer beard. It lives only on the island of Sumatra.

The Bornean orangutan and people

The name comes from Malay words meaning 'person of the forest'. Orangutans lose their homes when rainforest is cleared for timber and for oil palm plantations, and babies are sold illegally as pets after the mothers are killed. Rescue centres in Borneo raise orphans and return them to protected forest.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  5. Family Hominidae

Other members of the family Hominidae on this site: chimpanzee, Western gorilla.

More distant relatives in the order Primates: northern plains gray langur, rhesus macaque, ring-tailed lemur.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The Bornean orangutan is listed as Critically Endangered. The species faces an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild. This is the last category before "extinct in the wild".

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
