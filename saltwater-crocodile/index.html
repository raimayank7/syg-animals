Saltwater crocodile

Crocodylus porosus · Reptile · Carnivore

The saltwater crocodile is the largest living reptile. It lives in river mouths and mangrove swamps and can swim far out to sea. In India it is found in Bhitarkanika, the Sundarbans and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

What the saltwater crocodile eats

The saltwater crocodile eats whatever it can overpower: fish, crabs, turtles, birds, monkeys, wild pigs, deer and even buffalo. It waits at the water's edge with only eyes and nostrils showing, then bursts out, drags the animal under and rolls over and over to tear it apart.

How it lives

It lives in tidal rivers, mangrove swamps and coasts, and can swim long distances across open sea, which is how it has reached many islands. The female heaps up a mound of plants and mud, lays her eggs inside and guards it. She digs the young out when they call from inside the eggs and carries them to water.

How to tell it apart

It is the largest living reptile. Compared with the mugger it grows much bigger, has a longer and heavier head with two ridges running from the eyes towards the nose, and lives in salty or brackish water. The gharial's thin snout cannot be confused with either.

The saltwater crocodile and people

In India it lives in Bhitarkanika in Odisha, the Sundarbans and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. It is the crocodile most dangerous to people, and warnings near its waters must be taken seriously. It was hunted heavily for its skin until it was protected, and has since recovered well in Bhitarkanika and in northern Australia.

Is it in danger?

The saltwater crocodile is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
