Fish

Fish live in water and breathe through gills, which take oxygen from the water. Most are covered in scales and move with fins. Fish are cold-blooded. Sharks and rays have a skeleton of cartilage, while most other fish have a skeleton of bone. Whales and dolphins are not fish: they are mammals.

How to recognise a fish

  • Live in water all their lives
  • Breathe through gills
  • Fins for swimming
  • Most have scales and lay eggs
  • Cold-blooded

Sorted by scientific order

Within the group, scientists place animals in orders. Animals in one order share a common ancestor.

  • Cypriniformes: goldfish, rohu
  • Salmoniformes: Atlantic salmon
  • Perciformes: clownfish
  • Gymnotiformes: electric eel
  • Lamniformes: great white shark
  • Syngnathiformes: lined seahorse
  • Orectolobiformes: whale shark

What they eat

  • Herbivores: rohu
  • Carnivores: Atlantic salmon, electric eel, great white shark, lined seahorse, whale shark
  • Omnivores: clownfish, goldfish

How fish live

Water holds far less oxygen than air, and gills are built to collect it. A fish takes water in through the mouth and pushes it out over the gills, where fine blood vessels pick up the oxygen. Most bony fish also have a swim bladder, a bag of gas that lets them hang at any depth without effort. Sharks have none and tend to sink if they stop swimming. Along each side of a fish runs the lateral line, a row of sense organs that feel movements in the water. With it, a shoal can turn as one, and a fish can find its way in the dark.

Three kinds of fish

Bony fish, such as the salmon, rohu and goldfish, have a skeleton of bone and are by far the largest group. Sharks and rays have a skeleton of cartilage, the bendy material of our ears and nose. A third, small group, the jawless fish, includes the lampreys. Some fish live only in fresh water, some only in the sea, and a few, like the salmon, move between the two.

Often mistaken

Whales and dolphins are mammals, and their tails are set flat, while a fish's tail is upright. A jellyfish and a starfish are not fish at all but invertebrates. A seahorse does not look like a fish, yet it has gills and fins and is one. The electric eel is a fish but not a true eel.

Other groups: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, invertebrates.
