Birds

Birds are warm-blooded animals with feathers, a beak and two wings. All birds lay eggs with hard shells, and most can fly. Their bones are light and partly hollow. Birds that cannot fly, such as the ostrich and the penguins, use their strength for running or swimming instead. Birds are the living descendants of dinosaurs.

How to recognise a bird

  • Feathers: no other animals have them
  • A beak with no teeth
  • Lay hard-shelled eggs
  • Warm-blooded
  • Two wings and two legs

Sorted by scientific order

Within the group, scientists place animals in orders. Animals in one order share a common ancestor.

  • Passeriformes: common myna, house crow, house sparrow
  • Galliformes: chicken, Indian peafowl
  • Anseriformes: mallard, mute swan
  • Cuculiformes: Asian koel
  • Accipitriformes: bald eagle
  • Strigiformes: barn owl
  • Struthioniformes: common ostrich
  • Sphenisciformes: emperor penguin
  • Bucerotiformes: great hornbill
  • Phoenicopteriformes: greater flamingo
  • Coraciiformes: Indian roller
  • Falconiformes: peregrine falcon
  • Columbiformes: rock dove
  • Psittaciformes: rose-ringed parakeet
  • Apodiformes: ruby-throated hummingbird
  • Gruiformes: sarus crane

What they eat

  • Herbivores: mute swan, rock dove, rose-ringed parakeet
  • Carnivores: bald eagle, barn owl, emperor penguin, Indian roller, peregrine falcon
  • Omnivores: Asian koel, chicken, common myna, common ostrich, great hornbill, greater flamingo, house crow, house sparrow, Indian peafowl, mallard, ruby-throated hummingbird, sarus crane

How birds live

Flight shapes almost everything about a bird. Feathers give a light, strong surface for the wing. The bones are thin-walled, there are no heavy teeth and jaws, and eggs are laid one at a time and grow outside the body, so a female never carries much extra weight. Flying uses a great deal of energy, so birds eat often and choose rich food: seeds, fruit, nectar, insects, fish or meat. The shape of the beak tells you which. A sparrow has a short cone for cracking seeds, an eagle a hook for tearing, a flamingo a strainer and a hummingbird a fine tube.

Nests, eggs and chicks

Some chicks, such as those of the chicken, the mallard and the ostrich, hatch covered in down and can walk and feed within hours. Others, such as those of the sparrow, the crow and the owl, hatch blind and bare and must be fed in the nest for weeks. A few birds build no nest at all: the koel leaves its eggs in the nests of crows.

Often mistaken

A penguin and an ostrich cannot fly, but both are birds: they have feathers and lay hard-shelled eggs. A bat flies but is a mammal. Not every bird sings, and in many kinds only the male has bright colours.
