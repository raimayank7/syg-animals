Dromedary camel

Camelus dromedarius · Mammal · Herbivore

The dromedary is the one-humped camel. Its hump stores fat, not water. It can go many days without drinking, then take in a very large amount at once. Long eyelashes and nostrils that close keep out blowing sand. It is the animal of the Thar Desert.

What the dromedary camel eats

Camels eat dry grass, thorny bushes and salty plants that other livestock refuse. The mouth has a tough lining and a split upper lip that copes with thorns. The hump is a store of fat, not water, and it shrinks and flops over when the camel goes hungry for long.

How it lives

A camel can go for days without drinking and then take in a very large amount of water in a few minutes. It loses little water, as its body temperature can rise through the day before it starts to sweat. Long lashes, nostrils that close and wide padded feet are all answers to blowing sand.

How to tell it apart

One hump means a dromedary, the camel of hot deserts from North Africa to India. Two humps mean a Bactrian camel, the shaggier animal of the cold deserts of Central Asia. An easy way to remember: the letter D on its side has one hump, and the letter B has two.

The dromedary camel and people

The camel is the state animal of Rajasthan. It pulls carts, carries loads, serves with the Border Security Force and stars at the Pushkar fair. Camel numbers in India have fallen sharply as tractors and trucks take over the work. There are no truly wild dromedaries left, though tame ones gone wild roam Australia.
