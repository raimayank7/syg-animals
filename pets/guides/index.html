Guides for pet owners

Practical guides that apply to more than one kind of pet.

  • The first week with a new pet

    How to settle a new dog, cat, rabbit, bird or small pet at home: what to prepare, what to do on day one, and how to build trust through the first week.

  • Summer care for pets

    How to keep dogs, cats, rabbits, small pets, birds and fish safe in the Indian summer: shade, water, walk timings, cooling tips and the danger signs of overheating.

  • Monsoon care for pets

    Rainy season care for dogs, cats and small pets: drying after walks, damp bedding, ticks and fleas, safe food storage and exercise on days when it pours.

  • Fireworks and loud noise

    How to keep dogs, cats, birds and small pets calm and safe during Diwali fireworks, weddings and thunder: a safe room, walk timings and what not to do.

  • Pets and the law in India

    A plain guide to the rules for pet owners in India: wild animals you must not keep, registering a dog, pets in housing societies, and the duty not to be cruel.

  • Adopting a pet

    Why adoption is worth considering, where to adopt, the questions to ask a shelter or a seller, and the signs of an animal that has been raised responsibly.

  • Travelling with a pet

    How to prepare a dog, cat or small pet for a car journey, what to pack, how to choose a carrier, and what to arrange when the pet must stay behind.

How these guides fit with the pet pages

Each kind of pet on this site has its own page on housing, food and daily care. The guides here deal with the things that are the same for every animal in the house: a new arrival, the Indian summer and monsoon, festival noise, a journey, and what the law expects of an owner. Read the page for your own pet first, then the guide for the season or the event that is coming.

A calendar for an Indian home

April to June is the time for the summer guide: shade, water and no walks on hot roads. From the first rains, read the monsoon guide, since damp bedding, wet paws and stored food that spoils are the worries then. Before Diwali and wedding season, read the fireworks guide a few weeks ahead, because the things that help need practice. Before any trip, read the travel guide early enough to arrange papers or someone to look after the pet.

What the guides will not do

These are care guides. They do not name medicines or doses and do not try to work out what is wrong with a sick animal. For anything to do with illness, injury, vaccination or breeding, the person to ask is a vet who can examine your pet.
