Pets

Which pet suits you?

Answer six questions honestly. You will see which pets fit your home and routine, and why.

How to read the result

The guide compares your answers with what each pet needs in time, space, cost and company. It is a starting point for a family discussion, not a verdict. Read the full care guide of any pet you are considering, and if you can, spend time with one before deciding.

Three honest rules

  • If the time is not there, choose a pet that needs less of it, or wait.
  • A pet is never a surprise gift. The person who will care for it must choose it.
  • A child can help, but an adult is always the one responsible.

How this guide decides

Each answer is compared with what every kind of pet needs. A dog needs daily walks, company through the day and space, so it scores badly for a home that stands empty for long hours. Fish and a hamster ask for little time but give little to hold. A rabbit or a guinea pig is gentle but easily hurt by a very young child. The result lists the pets that fit your answers first, with the reason, and then the ones that do not fit, also with the reason. Nothing you choose is stored or sent anywhere.

Before you bring any pet home

  • Count the years. A dog or a cat can live well over ten years, and some birds far longer. Ask who will look after it when you move, marry, travel or fall ill.
  • Count the cost honestly. Food, a vet, vaccinations, a cage or tank, and care while you are away all cost money every year, not once.
  • Ask everyone in the house. A pet that one person wants and the others only put up with is rarely a happy one.
  • Find a vet first. Know where the nearest vet is, and when it is open, before the animal arrives.
  • Think of adoption. Shelters have healthy animals waiting for homes. See the guide to adopting a pet.

Animals that are not pets

Wild animals and birds do not belong in a home, however tame they seem when young, and keeping native wild species is against the law in India. If the guide suggests no pet at all for your present routine, that is a real answer too: it is kinder to wait a few years than to keep an animal you cannot give time to.
