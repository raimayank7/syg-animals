Sloth bear

Melursus ursinus · Mammal · Omnivore

The sloth bear is the shaggy black bear of the Indian subcontinent, with a pale V on its chest. It feeds mainly on termites and ants, sucking them up noisily through a gap in its front teeth, and is very fond of fruit and honey. Mothers carry their cubs on their backs.

What the sloth bear eats

The sloth bear is built to eat termites and ants. It rips open a mound with its long curved claws, blows away the dust and sucks up the insects with a noise that can be heard far off. Its two upper front teeth are missing, which leaves a gap to suck through, and it can close its nostrils. It also loves honey, mahua flowers and fruit such as jamun and ber.

How it lives

Sloth bears are active mostly at night and rest by day in caves or thick bushes. They do not sleep through the winter. One or two cubs are born in a den. Once they leave it, they ride on their mother's shaggy back, which very few other bears do. They stay with her for two years or more.

How to tell it apart

Look for a long, untidy black coat, a pale dusty muzzle, a white or cream V or Y on the chest and very long pale claws. The Asiatic black bear also has a white chest mark, but it has shorter, sleeker fur, large round ears and lives mainly in the hill forests of the Himalayas.

The sloth bear and people

For centuries sloth bears were caught as cubs and made to 'dance' on roadsides. India has banned the practice and the last such bears were moved to rescue centres. The sloth bear does not see or hear well. When surprised at close range it may attack instead of running away, so people in bear country make a noise as they walk.

Is it in danger?

The sloth bear is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
