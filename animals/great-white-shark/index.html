Great white shark

Carcharodon carcharias · Fish · Carnivore

The great white shark is the largest fish that hunts large prey, such as seals. Its skeleton is made of cartilage, not bone, and it grows new teeth all its life. Attacks on people are rare.

What the great white shark eats

Young great whites eat fish and rays. As they grow they turn to seals, sea lions, dolphins and the carcasses of whales. A great white often attacks from below at speed, sometimes leaping clear of the water with its prey. Its teeth are triangular with saw edges and are replaced all through its life.

How it lives

Great whites travel alone over very long distances, and some cross whole oceans. A shark detects a trace of blood in the water and also feels the weak electric fields of living things through pores on its snout. It keeps parts of its body warmer than the sea, which helps it to swim fast. The young are born live and fend for themselves.

How to tell it apart

A heavy torpedo-shaped body, a pointed snout, black eyes and a crescent tail are its signs. The back is grey and the belly white, with a sharp line between the two. The whale shark is spotted and has a wide flat mouth. A dolphin's tail is set flat and beats up and down, while a shark's is upright and sweeps from side to side.

The great white shark and people

Attacks on people are rare, and many seem to be mistakes in which a swimmer or surfer is taken for a seal. Far more sharks are killed by people than the other way round, on fishing lines, in nets and for their fins. The great white is protected in several countries.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

Is it in danger?

The great white shark is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
