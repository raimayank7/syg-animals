Horse

Equus caballus · Mammal · Herbivore

Horses were tamed thousands of years ago and changed how people travelled, farmed and fought. A horse can sleep standing up by locking its leg joints. Its eyes are set on the sides of its head, giving a very wide view.

What the horse eats

Horses eat grass and hay. A horse has a small stomach and a very long gut, so it needs to eat little and often, and it would graze for most of the day if left to itself. Working horses are also given grain. They need plenty of clean water and salt.

How it lives

Horses are herd animals and are uneasy alone. They can doze standing up, because their leg joints lock, but they lie down for deep sleep. A foal stands and feeds within an hour or two of birth and can keep up with the herd the same day. The eyes are set on the sides of the head, which gives a very wide field of view.

How to tell it apart

A donkey is smaller, with much longer ears, an upright mane and a tufted tail. A mule, the young of a male donkey and a female horse, falls in between. A horse's height is measured in hands at the shoulder. India's Marwari and Kathiawari horses are known by ears that curve inwards until the tips nearly touch.

The horse and people

Horses were tamed on the grasslands of Central Asia several thousand years ago, and they changed travel, farming and war. Today they are kept for riding, sport, the police and army, and tongas in some towns. The only truly wild horse left is Przewalski's horse of Mongolia.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Perissodactyla
  5. Family Equidae
  6. Genus Equus
  7. Species Equus caballus

Other members of the family Equidae on this site: donkey, plains zebra.

More distant relatives in the order Perissodactyla: Indian rhinoceros, white rhinoceros.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The horse is a domestic animal, kept and bred by people. Domestic animals are not assessed for the Red List of threatened species.
