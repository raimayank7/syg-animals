Common bottlenose dolphin

Tursiops truncatus · Mammal · Carnivore

Dolphins are small toothed whales. They are fast, playful and clever, live in groups, and find their way and their food by echolocation: making clicks and listening for the echoes. They breathe air through a blowhole.

What the common bottlenose dolphin eats

Bottlenose dolphins eat fish, squid and shrimp. They find prey by echolocation: they send out rapid clicks and listen to the echoes that come back. Groups often herd fish into a tight ball or drive them on to mudbanks, and some follow fishing boats for scraps.

How it lives

Dolphins live in pods that keep changing as animals come and go. Each dolphin has its own signature whistle, which works something like a name. A calf is born tail first and is helped to the surface for its first breath. Dolphins sleep with one half of the brain at a time, so they can keep surfacing to breathe.

How to tell it apart

A sturdy grey body, paler below, a short thick beak and a curved fin in the middle of the back describe this dolphin. Porpoises are smaller, with a blunt head and a triangular fin. On Indian coasts the dolphin seen close to shore is often the Indian Ocean humpback dolphin, which has a hump under its fin.

The common bottlenose dolphin and people

This is the dolphin of films and marine parks, and the best studied. Dolphins are mammals: they breathe air, are warm-blooded and feed their young on milk. In the wild they drown in fishing nets and are harmed by pollution. India does not allow dolphins to be kept for shows.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Cetacea
  5. Family Delphinidae
  6. Genus Tursiops
  7. Species Tursiops truncatus

Other members of the family Delphinidae on this site: orca.

More distant relatives in the order Cetacea: blue whale, Ganges river dolphin, humpback whale.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The common bottlenose dolphin is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
