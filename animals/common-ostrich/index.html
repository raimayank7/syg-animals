Common ostrich

Struthio camelus · Bird · Omnivore

The ostrich is the largest living bird and lays the largest eggs. It cannot fly, but it is the fastest runner among birds. It has only two toes on each foot. It does not bury its head in the sand.

What the common ostrich eats

Ostriches eat grass, leaves, seeds, roots and flowers, and now and then insects and small lizards. They have no teeth and swallow pebbles, which grind the food in the gizzard. They get most of their water from plants and can go several days without a drink.

How it lives

Ostriches live in small groups on open plains, often among zebra and antelope. Several hens lay in one shallow nest scraped by the male. The brown female sits by day and the black male by night, so each matches the light. Chicks can run soon after hatching. An ostrich does not bury its head in sand: it lays its neck flat on the ground to hide.

How to tell it apart

It is the largest and heaviest living bird and lays the largest egg. It is the only bird with two toes on each foot. The male is black with white wing and tail plumes, and the female is grey-brown. The emu of Australia and the rheas of South America are smaller and have three toes.

The common ostrich and people

An ostrich cannot fly, but it can run faster than any other bird and its kick can kill a lion. Ostriches are farmed for meat, leather and feathers. Their plumes were once so fashionable for hats that farming them made fortunes in South Africa.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The common ostrich is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
