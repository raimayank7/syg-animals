Red fox

Vulpes vulpes · Mammal · Omnivore

The red fox is the most widespread wild member of the dog family. It has a pointed face, upright ears and a bushy tail. Foxes eat almost anything, from mice and insects to fruit, and many now live in cities.

What the red fox eats

A red fox eats mice, voles, rabbits, birds, eggs, insects, earthworms, berries and fallen fruit, and in towns it picks through rubbish. It hunts mice by listening, then leaps high and comes down with its front paws on the spot. Spare food is buried and dug up later.

How it lives

Foxes are active mostly from dusk to dawn. They live as a pair or a small family in a burrow called an earth or den. Four or five kits are born in spring with dark, woolly fur and closed eyes. Both parents bring food. By autumn the young are full grown and most leave to find their own ground.

How to tell it apart

Look for the white tip to the long bushy tail, the black backs of the ears and the black lower legs, like stockings. The coat is usually rusty red but can be greyer or darker. A golden jackal is larger and sandier, with a shorter tail that has no white tip.

The red fox and people

The fox is the clever trickster of fables from Aesop to the Panchatantra. It was taken to Australia in the 1800s for sport and became a serious threat to small native animals there. In India the red fox lives in the Himalayas and Ladakh and in the deserts of Rajasthan and Kutch.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Carnivora
  5. Family Canidae
  6. Genus Vulpes
  7. Species Vulpes vulpes

Other members of the family Canidae on this site: dog, wolf.

More distant relatives in the order Carnivora: brown bear, cheetah, domestic cat, giant panda, jaguar, leopard, lion, polar bear, red panda, sloth bear, snow leopard, spotted hyena.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The red fox is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
