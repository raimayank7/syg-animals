Seven-spot ladybird

Coccinella septempunctata · Invertebrate · Carnivore

The seven-spot ladybird is a small red beetle with seven black spots. Its bright colour warns birds that it tastes bad. Both adults and larvae eat large numbers of aphids, so gardeners and farmers welcome them.

What the seven-spot ladybird eats

Ladybirds eat aphids, the small sap-sucking insects that crowd on tender shoots. Both the adult beetle and its larva hunt them, and one ladybird eats thousands in its life. When aphids are scarce they also eat other soft insects, pollen and nectar.

How it lives

The female lays clusters of yellow eggs near a colony of aphids. The larva is dark blue-grey with orange spots, long and bumpy, and looks nothing like its parents. It turns into a pupa stuck to a leaf, and the beetle comes out pale and gains its colour and spots within hours. Adults pass the cold season hidden in groups under bark or leaves.

How to tell it apart

The wing cases are red with seven black spots: three on each side and one shared across the middle, just behind the head. The number of spots tells the species and has nothing to do with age. The harlequin ladybird is more variable, with anything from no spots to many, and usually has brown legs and a white 'M' or 'W' mark behind the head.

The seven-spot ladybird and people

Farmers and gardeners count the ladybird as a friend because it controls aphids without chemicals. It is common on mustard and wheat crops in India. The bright colours warn birds that it tastes bad, and when handled it oozes a yellow, smelly liquid from its leg joints. In many countries it is thought to bring luck.
