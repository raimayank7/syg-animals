Cheetah

Acinonyx jubatus · Mammal · Carnivore

The cheetah is the fastest land animal over short distances. It has a slim body, long legs and black "tear lines" running from its eyes to its mouth. Unlike most cats it hunts by day and cannot fully pull in its claws, which grip the ground like running spikes. Cheetahs died out in India in the last century; animals brought from Africa have been released in Kuno National Park in Madhya Pradesh since 2022.

What the cheetah eats

Cheetahs hunt small and medium antelopes such as gazelles and impala, and also hares and the young of larger animals. A cheetah stalks to within easy reach, then sprints, trips the animal with a front paw and holds it by the throat. The chase is over in well under a minute. If it fails, the cheetah must rest before it can try again.

How it lives

Females live alone with their cubs. Males, often brothers, stay together for life in small groups. Cubs are born with a cape of long silvery hair down the back, which may help to hide them in grass. Many cubs are lost to lions and hyenas. Cheetahs cannot roar: they purr, chirp and hiss.

How to tell it apart

Look at the face first: two black lines run from the inner corner of each eye down to the mouth. The spots are solid and round, not rosettes. The body is light and deep-chested with a small head, long legs and a long tail that acts as a rudder in fast turns. A leopard is stockier and has no tear lines.

The cheetah and people

Rulers in India once kept tame cheetahs to hunt blackbuck, and the English word comes from the Hindi word chita, meaning spotted. Cheetahs are not strong enough to defend a kill, so they often lose food to other hunters, and they need very large open spaces. That makes them hard to protect in a crowded world.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Carnivora
  5. Family Felidae
  6. Genus Acinonyx
  7. Species Acinonyx jubatus

Other members of the family Felidae on this site: domestic cat, jaguar, leopard, lion, snow leopard, tiger.

More distant relatives in the order Carnivora: brown bear, dog, giant panda, polar bear, red fox, red panda, sloth bear, spotted hyena, walrus, wolf.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The cheetah is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
