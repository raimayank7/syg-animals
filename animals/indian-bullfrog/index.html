Indian bullfrog

Hoplobatrachus tigerinus · Amphibian · Carnivore

The Indian bullfrog is the large frog of the monsoon. In the breeding season males turn bright yellow with blue vocal sacs and call loudly from flooded fields. It eats insects, worms and even small mice and snakes.

What the Indian bullfrog eats

The bullfrog eats whatever moves and fits in its wide mouth: insects, worms, crabs, smaller frogs, mice, small snakes and even small birds. It sits and waits, then lunges and pushes the prey in with its front feet. Its tadpoles also eat other tadpoles.

How it lives

For most of the year bullfrogs live quietly by ponds, ditches and paddy fields and hide in holes through the dry months. The first heavy rain of the monsoon brings them out in numbers to breed in flooded fields. The females lay masses of eggs in the water, and the tadpoles grow fast before the pools dry up.

How to tell it apart

This is the largest frog in India, olive-green or brown with darker marks and often a pale line down the back. Breeding males turn bright lemon yellow with two deep blue throat pouches, which swell as they call. A toad has dry, warty skin and short legs, and it walks or makes short hops.

The Indian bullfrog and people

Frogs eat huge numbers of crop pests and mosquitoes. India once exported frogs' legs in such quantity that insect pests increased in the paddy fields, and the export was banned in 1987. The bullfrog has been carried to the Andaman Islands, where it now threatens local wildlife.

Is it in danger?

The Indian bullfrog is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
