Indian star tortoise

Geochelone elegans · Reptile · Herbivore

The Indian star tortoise has a high domed shell marked with yellow star patterns. The pattern breaks up its outline in dry grass. It is protected by law in India, and keeping or trading it is illegal.

What the Indian star tortoise eats

Star tortoises eat grass, fallen fruit, flowers and the leaves of juicy plants. They are most active in the monsoon, when fresh green food is plentiful. In the dry months they feed in the early morning and evening and shelter under bushes through the heat.

How it lives

This is a land tortoise of dry scrub and grassland and cannot swim. It lives alone and moves slowly over a small home area. The female digs a hole with her hind feet, lays a few eggs and covers them. The hatchlings get no care from their parents. Like other tortoises it can live for many decades.

How to tell it apart

Each plate of the high, domed shell has a yellow centre with yellow lines spreading out on a dark background, like a star. The pattern breaks up its outline among dry grass. Freshwater turtles such as the Indian flapshell have flatter shells and webbed feet. The red-eared slider sold in pet shops is a foreign water turtle.

The Indian star tortoise and people

Its beauty is its misfortune: the star tortoise is one of the most smuggled tortoises in the world, taken from the wild for the pet trade. In India it is illegal to catch, buy, sell or keep one. Many believe a tortoise brings luck to a house, but a wild tortoise kept indoors usually sickens and dies.

Is it in danger?

The Indian star tortoise is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.
