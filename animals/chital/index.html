Chital

Axis axis · Mammal · Herbivore

The chital, or spotted deer, is the most common deer of Indian forests. Its reddish coat is covered with white spots all year. Chital often feed below trees where langur monkeys drop leaves and fruit, and each warns the other of danger.

What the chital eats

Chital graze on short grass and also eat fallen fruit, flowers and leaves. They are often found under trees where langurs are feeding, picking up the leaves and fruit the monkeys drop. They drink at least once a day, so they stay near water.

How it lives

Chital live in herds, sometimes of a hundred or more, in forest clearings and at the forest edge. They are active in the morning and evening. The herd's sharp, high alarm call warns every animal within hearing that a tiger or leopard is about. Stags shed their antlers and grow a new pair each year.

How to tell it apart

The chital keeps its white spots for life, on a bright reddish-brown coat with a dark stripe down the back. The stag's antlers usually have three points on each side. The hog deer is smaller, darker and loses most of its spots as an adult. The sambar is much bigger and plain brown.

The chital and people

The chital is the most common deer of India's forests and the main food of tigers, leopards and wild dogs. A healthy herd of chital is a sign of a healthy forest. Its partnership with langurs is well known: the monkeys watch from the treetops while the deer listen and sniff the air below.

Is it in danger?

The chital is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
