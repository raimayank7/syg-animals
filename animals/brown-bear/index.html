Brown bear

Ursus arctos · Mammal · Omnivore

The brown bear is found across the northern half of the world. The grizzly bear of North America is one kind of brown bear. They eat roots, berries, insects, fish and meat, and sleep through the winter in a den after fattening up in autumn.

What the brown bear eats

Most of a brown bear's food is plants: grass, roots, bulbs, berries and nuts. It adds ants, moths, grubs, rodents, carrion and sometimes deer calves. On the coasts of Alaska and Kamchatka bears gather at rivers to catch salmon swimming upstream. In autumn a bear eats almost without stopping to build up fat for winter.

How it lives

Brown bears live alone, apart from mothers with cubs. In the cold months they sleep in a den and live off their fat, without eating or drinking. Cubs, usually two, are born in the den in midwinter, tiny and nearly hairless, and feed on rich milk while the mother sleeps. They stay with her for two or three years.

How to tell it apart

A brown bear has a clear hump of muscle over the shoulders, a face that dips between the eyes and the nose, and long front claws for digging. Colour is no guide, as it runs from pale gold to nearly black. The Asiatic black bear of the Himalayan forests has a white V on the chest and no shoulder hump.

The brown bear and people

The Himalayan brown bear lives above the treeline in Ladakh, Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand. Bears that learn to eat rubbish or crops lose their fear of people, and that is when accidents happen. In bear country people are told to store food safely and never to come between a mother and her cubs.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Carnivora
  5. Family Ursidae
  6. Genus Ursus
  7. Species Ursus arctos

Other members of the family Ursidae on this site: giant panda, polar bear, sloth bear.

More distant relatives in the order Carnivora: cheetah, dog, domestic cat, jaguar, leopard, lion, red fox, red panda, snow leopard, spotted hyena, tiger, walrus.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The brown bear is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
