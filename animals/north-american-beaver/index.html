North American beaver

Castor canadensis · Mammal · Herbivore

Beavers cut down trees with their strong front teeth and build dams of sticks and mud across streams. The pond behind the dam protects their home, called a lodge. Few animals change their surroundings as much.

What the North American beaver eats

Beavers eat the inner bark, twigs and leaves of trees such as aspen, willow and birch, as well as water plants. They do not eat fish. In autumn they pile branches under water near the lodge as a larder for the winter, when the pond is frozen over.

How it lives

A beaver family dams a stream with logs, sticks and mud to make a pond, and builds a dome-shaped lodge in it with entrances under the water. Beavers work mostly at night. They slap the water with the flat tail as an alarm before diving. Kits stay with their parents for about two years and help with repairs.

How to tell it apart

The flat, scaly, paddle-shaped tail is the clue. A beaver also has webbed hind feet and big orange front teeth, which never stop growing and are kept sharp by gnawing. A muskrat is much smaller, with a thin tail. An otter is long and slim, and it eats fish. The Eurasian beaver looks almost identical.

The North American beaver and people

Beavers were trapped almost to extinction for their fur, which was made into hats. Now they are valued as engineers: their ponds store water, slow floods and make homes for fish, frogs and birds. The beaver is a national symbol of Canada. Its dams can also flood roads and farmland.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Rodentia
  5. Family Castoridae
  6. Genus Castor
  7. Species Castor canadensis

More distant relatives in the order Rodentia: house mouse, Indian crested porcupine, Indian palm squirrel.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The North American beaver is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
