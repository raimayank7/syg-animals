Peregrine falcon

Falco peregrinus · Bird · Carnivore

The peregrine falcon is the fastest animal in the world when it dives on its prey from a height. It hunts other birds in flight and now nests on tall buildings in many cities.

What the peregrine falcon eats

The peregrine feeds almost wholly on other birds, caught in the air: pigeons, doves, ducks, waders and parakeets. It climbs high, folds its wings and dives on its prey. In this dive, called a stoop, it has been timed at more than 300 kilometres an hour, the fastest movement of any animal.

How it lives

Peregrines make no real nest. The eggs are laid in a bare scrape on a cliff ledge, and in cities on tall buildings, towers and bridges, where pigeons are plentiful. Pairs use the same ledge year after year. The female, called the falcon, is clearly larger than the male, called the tiercel.

How to tell it apart

Look for a dark hood with a broad black 'moustache' on a white cheek, a slate-grey back, a barred breast and long pointed wings. The shaheen, the peregrine that lives in India all year, is darker with rusty underparts. A kestrel is smaller and browner, and it hovers in one place, which a peregrine does not do.

The peregrine falcon and people

Falconers have prized the peregrine above other birds for thousands of years. Like the bald eagle it was nearly wiped out in Europe and North America by DDT in the mid-1900s and recovered after the poison was banned. It is found on every continent except Antarctica.

Is it in danger?

The peregrine falcon is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
