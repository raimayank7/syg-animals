Red kangaroo

Osphranter rufus · Mammal · Herbivore

The red kangaroo is the largest marsupial. It moves by hopping on powerful back legs, using its heavy tail for balance. A newborn is tiny and crawls into its mother's pouch, where it grows for months.

What the red kangaroo eats

Red kangaroos graze on grasses and low herbs, choosing fresh green shoots. Like cattle they have a stomach with chambers where microbes break down tough plants. They can go a long time without drinking if the plants they eat are green.

How it lives

Kangaroos rest in the shade through the heat and feed from late afternoon into the night. They live in loose groups called mobs. A newborn joey is about the size of a bean. It crawls unaided through its mother's fur into the pouch, fixes itself to a teat and grows there for several months before it first looks out.

How to tell it apart

The red kangaroo is the largest of all the marsupials. Males are reddish-brown and females are often blue-grey. Hopping on the huge hind legs with the tail for balance is a very efficient way to cover ground. The grey kangaroos are similar in build but live in wetter country. Wallabies are smaller relatives.

The red kangaroo and people

The kangaroo stands on Australia's coat of arms. People say it was chosen because it cannot easily move backwards. Kangaroos are common, and collisions with cars at night are frequent on country roads. Males settle disputes by leaning back on their tails and kicking, which looks like boxing.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Diprotodontia
  5. Family Macropodidae
  6. Genus Macropus
  7. Species Osphranter rufus

More distant relatives in the order Diprotodontia: koala.

Scientists do not always agree on names. The GBIF list files this animal under the name Macropus rufus.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The red kangaroo is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
