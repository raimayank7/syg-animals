Platypus

Ornithorhynchus anatinus · Mammal · Carnivore

The platypus is one of very few mammals that lay eggs. It has a bill like a duck, a tail like a beaver and webbed feet. It finds its food under water by sensing the tiny electric signals of its prey. Males have a venomous spur on each back leg.

What the platypus eats

A platypus hunts under water for insect larvae, shrimps, worms and small shellfish on the beds of rivers. It shuts its eyes, ears and nostrils when it dives. The soft bill picks up the faint electric signals given off by the muscles of its prey. It stores the catch in cheek pouches and chews it at the surface.

How it lives

The platypus lives alone in a burrow in the riverbank and is active mostly at dawn, dusk and night. It is one of a handful of mammals that lay eggs. The mother lays one to three soft eggs in a nesting burrow and curls around them. The young lap milk that oozes from patches of her skin, as she has no teats.

How to tell it apart

A bill like a duck's, a flat tail like a beaver's, webbed feet and thick waterproof fur make up an animal that cannot be confused with any other. Males have a sharp spur on each hind leg that can inject venom. Its only egg-laying relatives are the spiny echidnas.

The platypus and people

When the first skin reached Britain in 1799, scientists suspected a hoax made by sewing a duck's bill on to another animal. The platypus lives only in eastern Australia and Tasmania. It needs clean, steady rivers, and suffers from drought, dams, pollution and drowning in fishing traps.

Is it in danger?

The platypus is listed as Near Threatened. The species is not yet classed as threatened, but it is close and may become so soon.
