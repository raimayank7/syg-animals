Hippopotamus

Hippopotamus amphibius · Mammal · Herbivore

The hippopotamus spends the hot day in water and comes out at night to graze. Its eyes, ears and nostrils sit on top of its head, so it can breathe and watch while almost fully under water. Despite its calm look it is one of the most dangerous large animals in Africa.

What the hippopotamus eats

For all its huge jaws, the hippo eats grass. At dusk it leaves the water and walks along worn paths to grazing lawns, where it crops grass with its wide lips for several hours before going back to the river at dawn. It eats little for an animal of its size, because floating all day uses little energy.

How it lives

Hippos spend the day packed together in rivers and lakes, with only eyes, ears and nostrils above the surface. They cannot really swim or float. They walk or bounce along the bottom and come up to breathe every few minutes, even while asleep. Calves are born in shallow water and can suckle below the surface.

How to tell it apart

Nothing else has that barrel body, short legs and vast square muzzle. The skin is almost hairless and gives out an oily reddish fluid that protects it from sun and germs. People once thought hippos sweated blood. Their closest living relatives are not pigs but whales and dolphins.

The hippopotamus and people

Hippos are among the most dangerous large animals in Africa. They upset boats and charge people who come between them and the water. They are hunted for meat and for their teeth, which are sold as ivory. Hippos also feed rivers: the dung they drop in the water nourishes fish.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Mammalia
  4. Order Artiodactyla
  5. Family Hippopotamidae
  6. Genus Hippopotamus
  7. Species Hippopotamus amphibius

More distant relatives in the order Artiodactyla: Bactrian camel, blackbuck, chital, cow, dromedary camel, gaur, giraffe, goat, moose, nilgai, pig, reindeer.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The hippopotamus is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
