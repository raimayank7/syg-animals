Common toad

Bufo bufo · Amphibian · Insectivore

Toads have dry, warty skin and walk more than they hop. They spend most of the year on land and return to ponds to breed, laying their eggs in long strings.

What the common toad eats

Toads eat slugs, snails, beetles, ants, worms and spiders, caught with a flick of the sticky tongue. A toad sits still, watches its prey move, then strikes. It blinks as it swallows, because the eyeballs help to push the food down the throat.

How it lives

A toad spends the day under a log or stone and hunts at night, often returning to the same hiding place for months. It walks or crawls and seldom hops. In spring, toads travel back to the pond where they were born, sometimes in hundreds. They lay their eggs in long double strings wound round water plants. In cold countries they sleep through the winter.

How to tell it apart

Dry, warty skin, a squat body, short legs and copper-coloured eyes mark the toad. The swelling behind each eye is a gland that gives out a bitter poison if the toad is seized. A frog has smooth, moist skin and long legs, hops, and lays its eggs in clumps. The toad of Indian gardens is a different species, the Asian common toad.

The common toad and people

Toads do not give people warts. Gardeners value them as eaters of slugs. In parts of Europe volunteers run 'toad patrols' on spring nights, carrying toads across roads in buckets, and some roads have tunnels built for them. Dogs that mouth a toad soon learn to leave them alone.

Is it in danger?

The common toad is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
