Olive ridley sea turtle

Lepidochelys olivacea · Reptile · Omnivore

The olive ridley is a small sea turtle known for mass nesting, when many thousands of females come ashore together over a few nights. This event is called an arribada. The beaches of Odisha, such as Gahirmatha and Rushikulya, are among the most important in the world.

What the olive ridley sea turtle eats

Olive ridleys eat a mixed diet of crabs, shrimps, snails, jellyfish, small fish and some seaweed. They feed in the open ocean and in shallow coastal water and can dive deep to forage on the sea bed.

How it lives

This turtle is known for its mass nesting. On a few special beaches, thousands of females come ashore together over a few nights, each to dig a nest and lay about a hundred eggs. The event is called an arribada, a Spanish word meaning 'arrival'. Some weeks later the beach is alive with hatchlings making for the sea.

How to tell it apart

It is one of the smallest sea turtles, with a rounded, heart-shaped, olive-grey shell that is almost as wide as it is long. The green turtle is much larger, with a smoother, browner shell and a smaller head for its size.

The olive ridley sea turtle and people

The coast of Odisha has some of the largest mass-nesting beaches in the world, at Gahirmatha, Rushikulya and the mouth of the Devi river. Many turtles drown in trawl nets offshore, so fishing is restricted near these beaches in the nesting season. Volunteers on beaches from Chennai to Maharashtra protect nests and release hatchlings.

Is it in danger?

The olive ridley sea turtle is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.
