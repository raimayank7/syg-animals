European hedgehog

Erinaceus europaeus · Mammal · Insectivore

A hedgehog is covered in thousands of sharp spines. When frightened it rolls into a tight ball. It comes out at night to eat beetles, worms and slugs, and sleeps through the winter.

What the European hedgehog eats

Hedgehogs eat beetles, caterpillars, earthworms, slugs and snails, and now and then eggs or fallen fruit. They find food mostly by smell and hearing, snuffling noisily through leaves. In one night a hedgehog may walk more than a kilometre in search of food.

How it lives

A hedgehog sleeps by day in a nest of leaves and comes out after dark. When danger comes it tucks in its head and legs and tightens a band of muscle, turning itself into a ball of spines. In cold countries it sleeps through the winter. The young are born with soft white spines under the skin.

How to tell it apart

A coat of several thousand short stiff spines on the back, a pointed snout and a small round body make it easy to recognise. The spines are fixed and cannot be thrown. A porcupine is a large rodent with long quills and is no relation. India has its own hedgehogs, with larger ears, in the dry north-west and the south.

The European hedgehog and people

Gardeners welcome hedgehogs because they eat slugs and insects. Their numbers in Britain have dropped steeply, as hedges are removed, gardens are fenced and many animals die on roads. People who feed them are asked to put out water and meaty pet food, never milk, which upsets a hedgehog's stomach.

Is it in danger?

The European hedgehog is listed as Near Threatened. The species is not yet classed as threatened, but it is close and may become so soon.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
