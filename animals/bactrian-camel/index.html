Bactrian camel

Camelus bactrianus · Mammal · Herbivore

The Bactrian camel has two humps and a thick woolly coat for bitter winters. In India a small number live in the Nubra Valley of Ladakh, a reminder of the old Silk Route trade.

What the Bactrian camel eats

Bactrian camels eat almost any plant the cold desert offers: dry grass, thorny and bitter shrubs, even salty plants. When there is no open water they eat snow. As in the dromedary, the two humps hold fat. They stand firm when the camel is well fed and sag when it is not.

How it lives

This camel lives where summers are scorching and winters far below freezing. It grows a thick, woolly coat for winter and sheds it in large ragged sheets in spring. It walks with both legs of one side moving together, which gives the rolling gait of all camels. A calf is born after about thirteen months.

How to tell it apart

Two humps, a heavier body, shorter legs and a shaggy coat set it apart from the one-humped dromedary. The wild Bactrian camel of the Gobi is a separate and very rare species, slimmer and with smaller, pointed humps.

The Bactrian camel and people

Caravans of Bactrian camels carried silk, tea and spices along the Silk Road for many centuries. A small herd in the Nubra valley of Ladakh descends from animals left behind when that trade stopped, and today they give rides to tourists among the sand dunes at Hunder.
