Common starfish

Asterias rubens · Invertebrate · Carnivore

A starfish, or sea star, usually has five arms, with rows of tiny tube feet underneath. It can regrow a lost arm. It feeds on mussels by pulling the shells apart and pushing its stomach inside.

What the common starfish eats

The starfish eats mussels, clams, oysters and barnacles. It wraps its arms round a shell and pulls steadily with hundreds of tube feet until the shell opens a crack. Then it pushes its own stomach out through its mouth and into the shell, digests the animal there and draws the stomach back in.

How it lives

A starfish creeps along on rows of tiny tube feet that work by water pressure: it pumps sea water through its body where we have blood. It has no brain, but a simple eye spot at the tip of each arm tells light from dark. If an arm is lost, a new one grows. It sheds its eggs into the sea, and the young drift as plankton before settling.

How to tell it apart

Five arms round a central disc, a rough skin with a line of pale knobs along each arm and an orange or brownish colour describe the common starfish. Brittle stars have a small round disc with thin, snaky arms that break easily. Despite its name the starfish is not a fish. It is a relative of sea urchins, and many people now call it a sea star.

The common starfish and people

It lives on the coasts of the north-east Atlantic, from rock pools to deep water. Swarms of starfish can strip a bed of mussels or oysters, so shellfish farmers work to keep them out. A starfish in a rock pool should be left in the water, as it cannot breathe in air.
