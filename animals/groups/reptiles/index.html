Reptiles

Reptiles are cold-blooded animals with dry, scaly skin. They take their warmth from their surroundings, which is why lizards and crocodiles bask in the sun. Most lay leathery-shelled eggs on land. Snakes, lizards, turtles, tortoises and crocodiles are all reptiles.

How to recognise a reptile

  • Dry skin covered with scales or bony plates
  • Cold-blooded: body warmth comes from the surroundings
  • Breathe air with lungs
  • Most lay eggs on land

Sorted by scientific order

Within the group, scientists place animals in orders. Animals in one order share a common ancestor.

  • Squamata: common house gecko, Indian cobra, Indian python, king cobra, Komodo dragon, veiled chameleon
  • Crocodylia: gharial, mugger crocodile, saltwater crocodile
  • Testudines: green sea turtle, Indian star tortoise, olive ridley sea turtle

What they eat

  • Herbivores: green sea turtle, Indian star tortoise
  • Carnivores: gharial, Indian cobra, Indian python, king cobra, Komodo dragon, mugger crocodile, saltwater crocodile
  • Omnivores: olive ridley sea turtle
  • Insectivores: common house gecko, veiled chameleon

How reptiles live

A reptile does not burn food to keep warm. It moves into the sun to heat up and into shade or a burrow to cool down, and this is why reptiles are most common in warm countries. The benefit is that a reptile needs very little food: a large python or crocodile can go for weeks or months between meals, in places where a mammal of the same size would starve. Scaly, waterproof skin and eggs with a shell let reptiles live far from water, in deserts and dry scrub, where amphibians cannot survive. As a reptile grows, it sheds its outer skin, in one piece in snakes and in flakes in lizards.

Eggs and young

Most reptiles bury their eggs in sand, soil or rotting leaves and leave them to hatch by the warmth of the sun. In crocodiles and many turtles the temperature of the nest decides whether the young are male or female. Crocodile mothers guard the nest and carry the hatchlings to water, and the king cobra guards hers too. Some snakes and lizards give birth to live young.

Often mistaken

A salamander looks like a lizard but is an amphibian with moist skin and no scales. A turtle lives in water and a tortoise on land, but both are reptiles. Most snakes are not venomous, though it is never safe to handle a wild snake.
