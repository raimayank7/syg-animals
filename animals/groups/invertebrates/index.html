Invertebrates

Invertebrates are animals without a backbone. This is not one family but a huge collection of very different groups: insects, spiders, snails, worms, octopuses, jellyfish, starfish and many more. Well over nine out of every ten kinds of animal are invertebrates. Many have a hard outer covering instead of bones inside.

How to recognise an invertebrate

  • No backbone or inner skeleton of bone
  • Many have an outer shell or hard casing
  • Insects have six legs, spiders and scorpions eight
  • Most are small, but a few, like some squid and octopuses, grow large

Sorted by scientific order

Within the group, scientists place animals in orders. Animals in one order share a common ancestor.

  • Hymenoptera: garden ant, Western honey bee
  • Octopoda: common octopus
  • Forcipulatida: common starfish
  • Crassiclitellata: earthworm
  • Stylommatophora: garden snail
  • Araneae: house spider
  • Scorpiones: Indian red scorpion
  • Lepidoptera: monarch butterfly
  • Semaeostomeae: moon jellyfish
  • Coleoptera: seven-spot ladybird
  • Diptera: yellow fever mosquito

What they eat

  • Herbivores: earthworm, garden snail, monarch butterfly, Western honey bee
  • Carnivores: common octopus, common starfish, house spider, Indian red scorpion, moon jellyfish, seven-spot ladybird
  • Omnivores: garden ant, yellow fever mosquito

The main kinds of invertebrate

The animals on this page belong to several quite separate branches of the animal kingdom. Arthropods have a hard outer skeleton and jointed legs: insects with six legs, spiders and scorpions with eight, and crabs and prawns with ten or more. They grow by shedding the old skeleton. Molluscs have a soft body, often with a shell: snails, clams, and also the octopus, which has lost its shell. Annelids are worms with a body made of rings, such as the earthworm. Cnidarians, the jellyfish, corals and sea anemones, have stinging cells. Echinoderms, the starfish and sea urchins, are built on a plan of five.

Why they matter

Invertebrates do much of the unseen work of the living world. Bees and butterflies pollinate flowers. Earthworms, ants and beetles turn dead leaves and dung into soil. Ladybirds and spiders keep down insects that eat crops. Tiny drifting animals feed the fish of the sea, and corals build reefs. A few are harmful to people, such as the mosquitoes that carry disease, but life on land and in the sea could not go on without the rest.

Often mistaken

A spider is not an insect: count the legs. A termite, the "white ant", is not an ant. A starfish and a jellyfish are not fish. And a worm has no skeleton at all, inside or out.
