Mammals

Mammals are warm-blooded animals with hair or fur. Mothers feed their young on milk. Almost all mammals give birth to live young: the platypus and the echidnas, which lay eggs, are the exceptions. Mammals breathe air with lungs, even those that live in the sea, such as whales and dolphins. People are mammals too.

How to recognise a mammal

  • Hair or fur at some stage of life
  • Young are fed on their mother's milk
  • Warm-blooded: the body keeps a steady temperature
  • Breathe air with lungs
  • A backbone, and usually four limbs

Sorted by scientific order

Within the group, scientists place animals in orders. Animals in one order share a common ancestor.

  • Artiodactyla: Bactrian camel, blackbuck, chital, cow, dromedary camel, gaur, giraffe, goat, hippopotamus, moose, nilgai, pig, reindeer, sambar, sheep, water buffalo, wild boar, yak
  • Carnivora: brown bear, cheetah, dog, domestic cat, giant panda, jaguar, leopard, lion, polar bear, red fox, red panda, sloth bear, snow leopard, spotted hyena, tiger, walrus, wolf
  • Primates: Bornean orangutan, chimpanzee, northern plains gray langur, rhesus macaque, ring-tailed lemur, Western gorilla
  • Cetacea: blue whale, common bottlenose dolphin, Ganges river dolphin, humpback whale, orca
  • Perissodactyla: donkey, horse, Indian rhinoceros, plains zebra, white rhinoceros
  • Rodentia: house mouse, Indian crested porcupine, Indian palm squirrel, North American beaver
  • Proboscidea: African bush elephant, Asian elephant
  • Diprotodontia: koala, red kangaroo
  • Sirenia: dugong
  • Erinaceomorpha: European hedgehog
  • Lagomorpha: European rabbit
  • Chiroptera: Indian flying fox
  • Pholidota: Indian pangolin
  • Monotremata: platypus
  • Pilosa: three-toed sloth

How mammals live

Mammals have spread into almost every kind of place on Earth. Bats took to the air, whales and dolphins went back to the sea, moles live under the ground and camels cross deserts. What made this possible is a steady body temperature, which lets a mammal stay active through cold nights and winters, and the long care that mothers give their young. A young mammal learns from its mother: what to eat, where to find water and what to fear. Animals with long childhoods, such as elephants, apes and dolphins, are also the ones with the largest brains and the most complex family life.

Three ways of having young

Most mammals are placental: the baby grows inside the mother until it is well formed. Marsupials, such as the kangaroo and the koala, give birth to a tiny, unfinished baby that completes its growth in a pouch. The third and smallest group, the monotremes, lays eggs. Only the platypus and the echidnas do this, and their young still drink milk once they hatch.

Often mistaken

A bat is a mammal, not a bird: it has fur and feeds its young on milk. Whales, dolphins and the dugong are mammals, not fish: they come up to breathe air. The pangolin has scales but is a mammal too.

Other groups: birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, invertebrates.
