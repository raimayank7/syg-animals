Polar bear

Ursus maritimus · Mammal · Carnivore

The polar bear lives on the sea ice of the Arctic and hunts seals. Its fur looks white, but each hair is clear and hollow, and the skin beneath is black. A thick layer of fat keeps it warm. Melting sea ice is the main threat to its future.

What the polar bear eats

Polar bears live mainly on seals, above all ringed and bearded seals. A bear waits without moving beside a seal's breathing hole in the ice, or creeps up on seals resting on top. It eats the fat first, which gives the most energy. On land in summer it finds little to eat and lives on its stored fat.

How it lives

Polar bears wander alone over great distances and can swim for many hours. Under the fur the skin is black, and a thick layer of fat keeps out the cold. In autumn a pregnant female digs a den in a snowdrift. Her cubs, usually two, are born there in midwinter and come out in spring. They stay with her for more than two years.

How to tell it apart

It is the only bear that is white all over, though the fur can look cream or yellowish. Compared with a brown bear it has a longer neck, a narrower head, smaller ears and no shoulder hump. Its front paws are very broad, for paddling in water and for spreading its weight on thin ice.

The polar bear and people

Polar bears need sea ice as a platform for hunting, and the Arctic ice now melts earlier and forms later each year. That leaves the bears with a longer time ashore without food. Hungry bears wander into northern towns more often, and some towns now run patrols to move them away.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

Other members of the family Ursidae on this site: brown bear, giant panda, sloth bear.

More distant relatives in the order Carnivora: cheetah, dog, domestic cat, jaguar, leopard, lion, red fox, red panda, snow leopard, spotted hyena, tiger, walrus.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The polar bear is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.

This is the IUCN Red List category as recorded in Wikidata on 11 November 2026. The IUCN Red List itself is the authority and may carry a newer assessment. What the categories mean.
