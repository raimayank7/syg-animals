Indian roller

Coracias benghalensis · Bird · Carnivore

The Indian roller looks plain brown when perched, then shows brilliant blue wings in flight. It is named for the rolling dives of its display flight. Known as neelkanth in Hindi, it is the state bird of several Indian states.

What the Indian roller eats

The roller hunts from a perch. It sits on a wire, pole or bare branch, watches the ground and drops on grasshoppers, beetles, crickets, small lizards, frogs and scorpions. It gathers at grass fires to catch insects fleeing the flames, and comes to lights at night.

How it lives

Rollers are usually seen alone or in pairs in open country and farmland. The name comes from the breeding display, in which the bird flies up high and then tumbles and rolls downwards with harsh calls. It nests in a hole in a tree or wall and lays three to five white eggs.

How to tell it apart

Perched, it looks a dull brownish bird with a blue cap. When it opens its wings there is a flash of brilliant dark and pale blue bands. The European roller, which passes through north-west India in autumn, has a plain pale blue head and breast. A kingfisher has a long dagger-like bill.

The Indian roller and people

It is known as the neelkanth and is linked with Lord Shiva, and seeing one at Dussehra is thought lucky. Catching the birds to show them at the festival is illegal. It is the state bird of Odisha, Karnataka and Telangana. Farmers gain from it, since it eats many crop pests.

Is it in danger?

The Indian roller is listed as Near Threatened. The species is not yet classed as threatened, but it is close and may become so soon.
