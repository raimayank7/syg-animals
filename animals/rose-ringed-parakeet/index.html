Rose-ringed parakeet

Psittacula krameri · Bird · Herbivore

The rose-ringed parakeet is the bright green parrot seen across India. Males have a black and rose-pink ring round the neck. It is noisy, flies fast in flocks, and can learn to copy speech.

What the rose-ringed parakeet eats

Parakeets eat fruit, berries, seeds, grain, buds and flowers. Flocks descend on orchards and on fields of maize, sunflower and jowar, and they waste a good deal by taking a bite and dropping the rest. They hold food in one foot, as we hold it in a hand.

How it lives

These parakeets fly fast and straight in screeching flocks and gather in hundreds at evening roosts. They nest in holes in trees and walls, often enlarging an old woodpecker or barbet hole. They breed early, in the cool season, so the chicks hatch when trees are fruiting.

How to tell it apart

Only the adult male has the ring: a black line from the chin and a rose-pink band round the back of the neck. Females and young are plain green. The Alexandrine parakeet is larger and has a maroon patch on the shoulder. The plum-headed parakeet is smaller, and the male has a purplish-red head.

The rose-ringed parakeet and people

This is the 'tota' or 'mithu' of Indian homes and stories. Catching, selling or keeping it is illegal under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, like all native Indian birds. Escaped cage birds have formed wild flocks in London and many other cities of Europe.

Is it in danger?

The rose-ringed parakeet is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
