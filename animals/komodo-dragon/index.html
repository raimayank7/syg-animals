Komodo dragon

Varanus komodoensis · Reptile · Carnivore

The Komodo dragon is the largest lizard in the world. It is a kind of monitor lizard and can take prey as large as deer. It tracks food with its long forked tongue, which picks up scent from the air.

What the Komodo dragon eats

Komodo dragons eat deer, wild pigs, water buffalo and any dead animal they find. A dragon ambushes its prey and tears it with teeth that have saw-like edges. Glands in the lower jaw make venom that stops blood from clotting, so a bitten animal weakens. A dragon can eat a very large share of its own weight in one meal.

How it lives

Komodos live alone and are active by day, resting in burrows at night and in the hottest hours. The forked tongue picks up the scent of a carcass from kilometres away. Hatchlings climb trees at once and live there for their first years, safe from adults, which would eat them. Females kept without a male have produced young.

How to tell it apart

It is the largest lizard in the world: heavily built, grey-brown, with a long flat head, a thick tail and a yellow forked tongue. It is a giant monitor lizard. The Bengal monitor, the goh of Indian fields, belongs to the same family but is only a fraction of its size.

The Komodo dragon and people

Komodo dragons live only on Komodo, Rinca, Flores and a few small islands of Indonesia, inside or near Komodo National Park. Western scientists did not describe them until 1912. Attacks on people are rare but have happened. Their small range makes them vulnerable to fires, loss of prey and rising seas.

How it is classified

Scientists sort living things into groups within groups. Reading down, each step is a smaller group, ending with the species itself.

  1. Kingdom Animalia
  2. Phylum Chordata
  3. Class Reptilia
  4. Order Squamata
  5. Family Varanidae
  6. Genus Varanus
  7. Species Varanus komodoensis

More distant relatives in the order Squamata: common house gecko, Indian cobra, Indian python, king cobra, veiled chameleon.

Classification follows the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy. Other reference works arrange some groups differently. How classification works.

Is it in danger?

The Komodo dragon is listed as Endangered. The species faces a very high risk of extinction in the wild.
