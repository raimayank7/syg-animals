Yellow fever mosquito

Aedes aegypti · Invertebrate · Omnivore

Only female mosquitoes bite: they need blood to make eggs, while both sexes feed on plant juices. This striped mosquito breeds in small pools of clean standing water around homes and spreads dengue and chikungunya. Emptying water containers every week helps to control it.

What the yellow fever mosquito eats

Only the female mosquito bites. She needs the protein in blood to make her eggs, and she strongly prefers human blood. For energy, both males and females drink nectar and plant juices. The larvae live in water and feed on tiny scraps and microbes.

How it lives

This mosquito lives close to people and bites by day, mostly in the early morning and the late afternoon, often around the ankles. It lays its eggs just above the water line in small containers of fairly clean water: coolers, flower pots, buckets, tanks, coconut shells and old tyres. The eggs survive dry for months and hatch when the water rises. Egg to adult takes little more than a week in warm weather.

How to tell it apart

It is a small, dark mosquito with white bands on its legs and a white mark shaped like a lyre on its back. The Anopheles mosquito, which carries malaria, bites at night and rests with its tail end tilted up. The common brown Culex mosquito also bites at night and breeds in dirty water such as drains.

The yellow fever mosquito and people

This one insect spreads dengue, chikungunya, Zika and yellow fever. Since it breeds in and around homes, the standard advice from health departments is to empty, scrub and dry every water container once a week, cover water tanks, and clear away anything in which rain can collect. Full sleeves and mosquito repellent protect in the daytime.
