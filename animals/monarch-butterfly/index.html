Monarch butterfly

Danaus plexippus · Invertebrate · Herbivore

The monarch is an orange and black butterfly famous for its migration: every autumn millions fly thousands of kilometres from the United States and Canada to spend winter in Mexico. Its caterpillars feed on milkweed, which makes them poisonous to birds.

What the monarch butterfly eats

The caterpillar of the monarch eats only milkweed plants. Milkweed contains poisons, which the caterpillar stores in its body and keeps as an adult, so birds that try one are sick and learn to leave monarchs alone. The adult butterfly drinks nectar from many kinds of flower.

How it lives

The monarch goes through four stages: egg, caterpillar, pupa and butterfly. It is famous for its migration. Each autumn, monarchs from Canada and the United States fly thousands of kilometres to a few mountain forests in Mexico, where they cluster on the trees in millions. The return north in spring is completed over several generations.

How to tell it apart

The wings are bright orange with black veins and a black border dotted with white. The caterpillar is ringed in black, yellow and white. India's striped tiger butterfly, a close relative, looks very like it, and the plain tiger is similar without the black veins. Both also feed on milkweeds as caterpillars and are also distasteful to birds.

The monarch butterfly and people

The monarch is one of the best-known insects in North America and is studied by school children who tag and count them. Its numbers have fallen, as milkweed is cleared from farmland with weedkillers and the winter forests in Mexico are logged. Many people now plant milkweed in gardens to help.

Is it in danger?

The monarch butterfly is listed as Least Concern. The species is widespread or numerous enough that it is not considered at risk of extinction at present.
