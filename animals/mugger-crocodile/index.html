Mugger crocodile

Crocodylus palustris · Reptile · Carnivore

The mugger, or marsh crocodile, has a broad snout and lives in freshwater across the Indian subcontinent. It basks on banks to warm up. The mother guards her nest and carries the hatchlings gently to the water in her mouth.

What the mugger crocodile eats

Muggers eat fish, frogs, turtles, water birds and mammals that come to drink. Young ones start on insects and small fish. A mugger floats low and still, then lunges. It cannot chew, so it swallows small prey whole and tears larger animals apart by gripping and spinning.

How it lives

Muggers bask on banks in the morning, often with the mouth held open to lose heat. In dry seasons they dig burrows in the bank and may walk long distances overland to find water. The female buries her eggs in a hole in sand, guards the spot and carries the hatchlings to the water in her mouth. The warmth of the nest decides whether the young are male or female.

How to tell it apart

The mugger has the broadest snout of India's three crocodilians, and looks rather like an alligator. The gharial has a very long, thin snout. The saltwater crocodile grows much larger, has a longer head and lives in tidal creeks and mangroves, while the mugger keeps to fresh water: rivers, lakes, reservoirs and village tanks.

The mugger crocodile and people

The mugger is the most widespread crocodile in India and sometimes lives right beside people, as in the Vishwamitri river in the city of Vadodara. It can be dangerous, and warning boards are put up where it lives. The Madras Crocodile Bank near Chennai has bred muggers in thousands.

Is it in danger?

The mugger crocodile is listed as Vulnerable. The species faces a high risk of extinction in the wild.
