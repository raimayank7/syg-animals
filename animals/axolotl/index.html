Axolotl

Ambystoma mexicanum · Amphibian · Carnivore

The axolotl is a salamander that keeps its feathery gills and stays in water all its life, never taking the adult land form. It can regrow lost limbs. It is popular in aquariums but is close to extinction in the wild.

What the axolotl eats

The axolotl eats worms, insect larvae, small shellfish, tadpoles and tiny fish. It hunts mostly by smell at night and feeds by suction: it opens its mouth suddenly and the prey is swept in with a gulp of water.

How it lives

Most salamanders begin as water-living larvae with gills and then change into land animals. The axolotl never makes the change. It keeps its feathery gills and tail fin, stays in the water all its life and breeds in this young-looking form. It can regrow a lost leg, its tail and even parts of its heart and eyes without a scar.

How to tell it apart

A broad flat head with a permanent 'smile', three feathery gills on each side and a finned tail make it easy to know. Wild axolotls are dark brown or olive with speckles. The pale pink ones with red gills are a colour form bred in captivity. A tadpole of a frog has no visible legs or feathery gills at first, and soon changes shape.

The axolotl and people

In the wild the axolotl lives only in the canals of Xochimilco, at the edge of Mexico City, where pollution and introduced fish have made it extremely rare. Yet it is common in laboratories and home aquariums around the world, as scientists study its power of regrowth. The name comes from the language of the Aztecs.

Is it in danger?

The axolotl is listed as Critically Endangered. The species faces an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild. This is the last category before "extinct in the wild".
